Concrete - Design stage

Page 13 of 56 - Materials

This section defines how material information is captured within the BIM model using structured property sets. Material data provides the physical, mechanical, and performance characteristics required for analysis, design verification, fabrication, and lifecycle asset management. These properties should be applied consistently to ensure that material behaviour is correctly represented across all relevant structural elements.

This section defines the required material properties for structural concrete at the design stage. The information captures mechanical, durability, and classification parameters used to support structural analysis, compliance with design standards, and specification of concrete performance.

These properties are primarily mapped to IFC material and element property sets, including Pset_ConcreteElementGeneral, Pset_MaterialCommon, and Pset_MaterialMechanical.

Properties are assigned to IfcMaterial through Pset_ConcreteElementGeneral, Pset_MaterialCommon, and Pset_MaterialMechanical as indicated in the References column. Use the typed measures named in the Definition column where the schema and authoring tool support them; use IfcReal only as a documented fallback with units resolved through IfcUnitAssignment.

Exposure ClassNecessary
A classification that defines the environmental conditions to which a concrete element will be exposed during its service life. These classes specify the type and severity of exposure to factors such as carbonation, chlorides, freeze–thaw cycles, or chemical attack. They are used to determine the durability requirements for concrete, such as minimum cement content, maximum water/cement ratio, and cover to reinforcement.
ExistingPset_ConcreteElementGeneral → ExposureClassIfcLabel“XC4”
Manufacturing TechnologyNecessary
The method by which the concrete element is produced and constructed.
Typical examples include:
• In-situ – Concrete is poured and cured on site in its final position.
• Pre-cast – Concrete elements are manufactured in a controlled factory environment and later transported and installed on site.
• Pre-tensioned – Concrete is cast with pre-applied tension in the reinforcement to improve performance under load.
• Post-tensioned – Concrete is cast with tension later applied to the reinforcement to improve performance under load.
This classification affects quality control, dimensional accuracy, and structural design considerations.
ExistingPset_ConcreteElementGeneral → CastingMethodPEnum_ConcreteCastingMethod“Precast”
Mass DensityNecessary
The mass per unit volume of concrete, typically expressed in kg/m³. For normal-weight concrete, the nominal value is around 2400–2500 kg/m³, depending on the aggregate type and mix proportions. It is important for calculating self-weight, dead loads, and dynamic properties, and may also help identify lightweight or heavyweight concrete mixes.
ExistingPset_MaterialCommon → MassDensityIfcMassDensityMeasure
(or IfcReal)		2500 [kg/m³]
Strength GradeNecessary
The strength grade is fundamental for structural design, influencing section capacity, reinforcement requirements, and durability performance. The classification that defines the characteristic compressive strength of concrete is typically denoted as C x/y (e.g., C30/37).
• The first number (x) represents the cylinder strength in MPa.
• The second number (y) represents the cube strength in MPa.
ExistingPset_ConcreteElementGeneral → StrengthClassIfcLabel“C30/37”
Quality Control ClassNecessary
A classification that defines the level of production control and testing regime applied during concrete manufacture, The class selected affects the required frequency of sampling, testing, and compliance checks.
CustomPset_ConcreteElementGeneral → QualityClassIfcLabel“Class 1”
SpecificationNecessary
A formal set of technical requirements defining the materials, properties, performance, and workmanship standards to be achieved in the concrete or structure.
It includes aspects such as:
• Strength class and exposure class
• Workability, maximum aggregate size, and admixtures
• Durability and cover requirements
• Testing and acceptance criteria
Specifications ensure that the designed performance and durability are achieved in construction.
CustomPset_ConcreteElementGeneral → SpecificationIfcLabel“SPE-01-V1”
Poisson RatioOptional
A measure of the lateral strain to longitudinal strain in a material under uniaxial stress. It indicates how much concrete contracts laterally when stretched or expands laterally when compressed. This parameter is used in finite element analysis (FEA) and elastic stress–strain calculations.
• For uncracked concrete, a typical value is ν = 0.2.
• For cracked concrete, lateral strain is negligible, so ν ≈ 0.0.
ExistingPset_MaterialMechanical → PoissonRatioIfcPositiveRatioMeasure
(or IfcReal)		0.2

Thermal Expansion CoefficientOptional
A measure of how much concrete expands or contracts with temperature changes. It is expressed as the change in length per unit length per degree of temperature change. This property is important for assessing thermal stresses and designing expansion joints. Typical values for normal-weight concrete range from 13×10-6 to 14×10-6 m/m/°C

Also known as the modulus of elasticity, it defines the relationship between stress and strain in the linear-elastic range of concrete behaviour. note

Strength Grade and Exposure Class are typically IfcLabel values per the project specification. Quality Control Class identifies the conformity system (for example, 1, 2, or 3) and should align with the notation in the governing concrete standard. Mass Density, Shear Modulus, and related mechanical fields use the typed measures in the Definition column. Specification should reference the project concrete specification document or clause, not repeat mix proportions held elsewhere.
